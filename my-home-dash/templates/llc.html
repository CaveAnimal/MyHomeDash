Starting an anonymous LLC.

A privacy-focused LLC keeps your name off public records. It is not hidden from the IRS, your bank, or a court.

Back to Home
General information, not legal or tax advice. Fees and rules change; confirm each figure on the state's own website, and consider a one-hour consult with a business attorney before filing.

What "anonymous" really means

  • Public anonymity: member/owner names do not appear in the state's searchable records. Achieved by choosing a privacy-friendly state and using a registered agent.
  • Not anonymous to the IRS: you still need an EIN, and the "responsible party" must be a real person with an SSN or ITIN.
  • Not anonymous to banks: they must verify owners under federal know-your-customer rules.
  • Not a shield for wrongdoing: courts can pierce the LLC for fraud, commingled funds, or ignored formalities.
  • Federal ownership reporting: FinCEN's Beneficial Ownership (BOI) rule was narrowed in 2025 to exempt U.S.-formed companies. Verify the current status at fincen.gov before you file.

If you operate in another state (where you live or work), you generally must also register there as a foreign LLC, which can publish more information. For a local business, forming in your home state is often simpler even if it is less private.

Step by step

  1. Choose the state using the table above.
  2. Pick a name ending in "LLC" and search the state's business-entity database.
  3. Engage a registered agent (typically $50–$150/yr). Use their address, not your home.
  4. File the Articles of Organization online with the Secretary of State, listing the agent and a business mailing address.
  5. Draft the operating agreement naming the members. It stays internal.
  6. Apply for an EIN on irs.gov. It is free; ignore sites that charge.
  7. Open a bank account with the formation documents and EIN letter.
  8. Stay compliant: annual reports, taxes, and separate books.

Extra privacy options

  • Virtual mailbox or business address instead of your home on public filings.
  • Business phone number (VoIP) rather than your personal one.
  • Domain WHOIS privacy for the company website.
  • Nominee or manager-managed structure via an attorney: more complex, and still disclosed to banks and the IRS.

Rough first-year cost

New Mexico: about $50 filing + ~$100 agent = ~$150. Wyoming: about $100 + ~$100 + ~$60 = ~$260. Add a virtual address ($10–$30/mo) if wanted.
